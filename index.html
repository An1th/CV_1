Research Experience

PhD Thesis
2021 – 2026

Probing Clouds and Hazes in Hot-Jupiter Atmospheres from NUV–MIR

Investigated the atmospheric properties of hot and ultra-hot Jupiters using multi-wavelength observations from the near-ultraviolet to the mid-infrared, combining data from HST, JWST, VLT, Gemini, TESS, and ground-based facilities.

Applied forward modelling, Bayesian atmospheric retrievals, and photochemical models to constrain molecular abundances, clouds, hazes, thermal structures, and disequilibrium chemistry. The thesis also examined transit-timing variations and orbital evolution, investigating how atmospheric composition and planetary properties are influenced by gravity, irradiation, aerosols, and chemical processes.

Advisors: Dr. Carlos Ferreira Lopes, Dr. Amelia Bayo and Dr. Jeremy Tregloan-Reed

M.Sc. Thesis
2018 – 2019

Long-Term Study of Meteor Count Rate and Meteor Echo Detection Height During Solar Cycles 23 and 24

Analysed meteor radar observations from Thumba, India, to study meteor occurrence rates, echo-height variations, and ablation altitudes across Solar Cycles 23 and 24.

Examined correlations between meteor activity, sunspot number, solar radio flux at 10.7 cm (F10.7), and Mg II indices, while also investigating annual meteor-shower periodicity and atmospheric conditions in the Mesosphere and Lower Thermosphere region.

Advisors: Dr. Chenna Reddy and Dr. C. Ramathilagam
