PhD Thesis

Probing Clouds and Hazes in Hot-Jupiter Atmospheres from NUV–MIR

PhD thesis focused on the atmospheric and dynamical characterization of transiting exoplanets, particularly hot-Jupiters, using multi-wavelength transmission spectroscopy from optical to mid-infrared wavelengths. Combined ground- and space-based observations from facilities including HST, JWST, VLT, Gemini, and TESS to investigate atmospheric chemistry, clouds, hazes, and disequilibrium processes such as vertical mixing and photochemistry. Developed and applied forward modeling and Bayesian atmospheric retrieval techniques using POSEIDON to constrain molecular abundances, thermal structures, and aerosol properties of exoplanetary atmospheres

Projects

PhD Course Work – Extra-Solar Planets :

September 2022 – January 2023 | Universidad de Atacama, Copiapó, Chile

Report Title: Comparing the Photometric Parameters of the TESS Targets with its Literature Papers

Used the Python package Lightkurve to generate light curves of TESS targets. A model curve was fitted using the JKTEBOP code by performing 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations. The final parametric values were obtained through MCMC analysis and compared with values reported in the literature.

PhD Course Work – Scientific Writing

April 2022 – June 2022 | Universidad de Atacama, Copiapó, Chile

Report Title: Outlier Quiescent Galaxies with Less Hα Emission

Studied three galaxy clusters, A1914, A963, and RXJ1720, at redshifts 0.15 ≤ z ≤ 0.2 using data from the Arizona Cluster Redshift Survey. The project examined the spatial distribution of star-forming galaxies and investigated how stellar mass varies with Dn4000, Hα emission, specific star-formation rate, and stellar mass indicators. The report highlighted young galaxies with high star-formation rates within the studied clusters.

PhD Course Work – Astrophysics of the Galaxy

April 2022 – June 2022 | Universidad de Atacama, Copiapó, Chile

Report Title: Bimodality Distribution of the Galaxies

Investigated the bimodality of early- and late-type galaxies using parameters from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey Data Release 8. The project discussed the bimodality of color distribution, Sérsic index, star-formation rate, stellar mass, and absolute magnitude in the r-band. Contour plots were produced to distinguish early- and late-type galaxy populations.

M.Sc. Final Year Project

November 2018 – January 2019 | Osmania University, Hyderabad, India

Title: Long-Term Study of Meteor Count Rate and Meteor Echo Detection Height During Solar Cycle 23 & 24

Used SKiYMET Meteor Radar data to study meteor occurrence, velocity, location, meteor echo height variation, solar radio flux at F10.7, and Mg II during solar cycles 23 and 24. MATLAB was used to generate the results. The project also investigated maximum meteor ablation height, meteor shower periodicity, and variations in meteor activity with Sun Spot Number during each year.
